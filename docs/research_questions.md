# Research questions and precedents

Reviewed 2026-10-14. The project's vehicle of interest is the NCDB `V_TYPE=08` road tractor (with or without semitrailer). The outcome of interest is a **unique collision** involving one or more such vehicles. The public NCDB extract covers fatal and injury collisions, so every national result from it must be labeled *casualty collisions*. The intended ten completed calendar years are 2016–2025; source coverage must be shown explicitly rather than silently shortening the question.

### Cross-check of the supplied Claude Code exchange

- **Historical decline, with a narrower conclusion.** Transport Canada's tractor-trailer Table 1 totals fell from 3,300 casualty collisions in 2012 to 2,276 in 2021. Fatal collisions separately fell from 197 to 169, while injury collisions fell from 3,103 to 2,107. The fatal *share among casualty collisions* rose from 6.0% to 7.4% because injury collisions declined faster. This is a severity-mix signal, not evidence that tractor driving became more deadly per kilometre or that fatal collisions increased. It does not resolve 2016–2025.
- **Local reconstruction needs reconciliation.** A fresh read of `NCDB_1999_to_2017.csv`, grouping unique `C_CASE` when any row has `V_TYPE=08`, exactly matched published totals in 2012 (3,300) and 2014 (3,427), but differed by 1–2 collisions in 2013 and 2015–2017. The local `test_2019_dataset_en.csv` yields **2,871** such events (192 fatal, 2,679 injury) after restoring stripped leading zeros; Transport Canada's table has **2,777** (196 fatal, 2,581 injury). Fresh official English and French records reproduce the same difference. It remains unresolved, so any descriptive public-release trend must show the benchmark alternative and period sensitivity; it cannot be described as a fully reconciled current national series.
- **Do not mix fatal vehicle and collision series.** The Transport Canada S8 range of 179–239 for 2018–2023 refers to tractor-trailer **vehicles involved in fatal collisions**, not unique fatal crashes. Its 2023 count is estimated; some provincial inputs are preliminary.
- **Recorded factors are a source-access priority.** The public local NCDB files and current online selector lack the driver-action fields used in Transport Canada's fact sheet. [NCDB support](https://wwwapps2.tc.gc.ca/saf-sec-sur/7/ncdb-bndc/p.aspx?c=100-5-0&l=en) lists `NCDB-BNDC@tc.gc.ca` for questions. Ask whether a research extract or defined tabulation can be supplied, including coding and missingness by year; no access has yet been confirmed.

Show annual counts, absolute and percentage changes, and a count-model trend with uncertainty. For 2022–2025, obtain updated estimates before extending rates. Registrations are a weaker context measure. |
| 3 | **Who bears the harm when a road tractor is involved, and has that changed?** Identify whether safety outcomes improved for truck occupants and other road users alike. | All deaths and injuries in each tractor-involved event, broken out into tractor occupants, other vehicle occupants, pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists; also fatalities per 100 tractor-involved casualty collisions. | Linked person/vehicle/collision records. Check whether the public release includes severity and road-user codes consistently across years. Counts do not attribute fault. |
| 4 | **Are fatal events changing differently from injury events?** Locate a possible severity shift even if total casualty collisions fall. | Fatal collision share among tractor-involved casualty collisions; annual fatal and injury counts separately. Compare with heavy unit trucks (`07`) and all casualty collisions as contextual series. | Comparable event and vehicle codes. The conditional fatal share is not the probability of a fatal collision per trip. |
| 5 | **Which crash configurations contribute most to serious outcomes?** Identify patterns suitable for road-design or enforcement follow-up. | Distribution of single versus multi-vehicle, rear-end, head-on, angle, sideswipe, and other configurations; fatal share within each. Compare periods and with straight trucks. | Configuration codes and consistent cross-year definitions; collision counts, not person rows. |
| 6 | **How do weather, road surface, light, month, weekday, and hour relate to collision severity?** Find conditions with a high concentration of severe events. | Counts and fatal share within each condition, with unknowns shown. Standardize comparisons for year and available geography. | NCDB fields exist locally for weather/surface/time and road alignment; full jurisdiction and factor detail may require provincial data. A large share of crashes in clear weather or daytime does **not** imply these conditions are riskier without traffic exposure by condition. |
| 7 | **Did the mix of tractor crashes change after 2020?** Identify which event types or road users explain a trend. | Decompose the change in fatal and injury counts by configuration, season/time, and victim group; present both component counts and shares. | Complete event series; stable coding and enough observations. Interpret as contribution to the observed count change, not causal explanation. |
| 8 | **Do provinces show the same pattern?** Identify where national changes concentrate. | Jurisdiction-year tractor-event counts, severity and possibly rates with jurisdiction-specific tractor exposure. | Provincial collision-level files or an NCDB extract containing jurisdiction. ### Additional questions raised by the cross-review

| Question | What can be answered now | Data or design gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did an apparent Ontario 2022 truck-collision rise occur in other jurisdictions? | Compare labeled Ontario/OPP and national published series as a descriptive signal. | The OPP, Ontario ministry and NCDB cover different roads, vehicles, and outcomes. Province-coded tractor event records are needed for a comparable test. |
| Are older road tractors associated with more severe outcomes? | Among reported casualty collisions, compare fatal share by `collision year − vehicle model year`, adjusting for crash setting and year. | This is **severity conditional on a collision**, not a crash rate for older trucks. To test overrepresentation in crashes, obtain fleet age and distance driven by model year. Audit missing/invalid model years. |
| In car–tractor collisions, what maneuvers preceded impact for each vehicle? | Descriptive distributions may be possible where both vehicles' maneuvers are recorded; the local public extract lacks maneuvers, while the online tool shows a selected-vehicle maneuver. | Linked vehicle-level records with both maneuvers are needed. Maneuver and impact point do not establish legal fault; the OPP 86% news figure is not a national benchmark. |
| Did driver training, speed limiters or electronic logs change safety? | Document policy timing and plot pre/post trends as background. [Ontario's Class A mandatory training began July 2017](https://news.ontario.ca/en/bulletin/40926/ontario-first-in-canada-to-introduce-mandatory-entry-level-training-for-class-a-truck-drivers); [electronic-log enforcement followed a transition through January 2023](https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2022-10/electornic-logging-devices-commercial-vehicles-factsheet_r14.pdf). | **Do not infer effects from before/after counts alone.** A credible evaluation needs jurisdiction-specific policy timing and adherence, tractor exposure, untreated/less-exposed comparisons, pretrend checks, and enough post-policy years. Ontario's earlier speed-limiter rule predates the proposed decade, and 2023 is too close to the electronic-log transition for a strong national estimate. |
| Does truck-driver experience matter? | The [Insurance Bureau of Canada summarizes a commissioned training study](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/urgent-action-needed-for-commercial-truck-driver-training) reporting greater collision involvement for drivers with less than three years of experience. | The public NCDB files do not record years of commercial driving experience. Testing this requires linked licensing/insurance records, driver-level exposure, and an exact definition of experience. Do not substitute driver age for experience. |
| Do freight activity and tractor collision counts move together? | Compare annual directional trends with published activity series. | Freight volume/tonne-kilometres are not tractor VKT and can change with payload or trip length. Use as context or a modeled covariate only after geography, vehicle class, and revisions are checked. |

## Analysis rules

1. Keep four units separate: **collision**, **vehicle involvement**, **person harmed**, and **exposure**. A person-level NCDB row count is none of the first three without grouping.
2. Use `V_TYPE=08` as the primary definition. Publish `07` and `07+08` only as labeled sensitivity comparisons; a Class 8 registration or broad `heavy vehicle` category is not equivalent.
3. Start with observed annual counts and their uncertainty. A ten-point annual series supports modest trend claims; avoid elaborate forecasting or causal models without richer data.
4. State property-damage-only exclusions for NCDB results. A provincial all-collision series may be shown separately if its definition and reporting threshold are stable.
5. Audit unknown/missing values and source revisions by year before interpreting any shift. Published 2023 tractor fatal-involvement data are estimated, with some provincial inputs preliminary.
6. Treat weather, time, driver attributes, and recorded actions as **associations** unless an appropriate exposure or causal design is available. Do not infer driver fault from truck involvement.

## Proposed first analysis deliverable

Build one reproducible national **casualty-collision trend** through the latest year for which tractor-specific collision-level records are actually obtainable. Include a one-page metric dictionary, annual fatal/injury event table, trend chart, victim-group table, 2020 sensitivity comparison, and reconciliation against Transport Canada's published tractor-trailer counts. Add 2024–2025 as a clearly separated provincial evidence appendix until event-level national coverage and comparability are established.
