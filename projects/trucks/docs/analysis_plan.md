# Analysis plan: Canadian road tractor collisions

## Question and scope

**Primary question:** Did police-reported collisions involving a road tractor increase over the last decade? As of October 2026, the target for ten **completed calendar years** is **2016–2025**. Available collision microdata cover 1999–2021, so the first descriptive analysis uses **2012–2021** as an explicitly historical decade and **2016–2021** as the observed portion of the target. The live NCDB Online tool extends vehicle-involvement tables to 2023; a separate 2014–2023 comparison would use that different measure. Treat 2026 as a year-to-date question only if comparable partial-year data are available. State the period and counting unit with every result.

**Primary vehicle definition:** NCDB `V_TYPE=08`, “road tractor, with or without a semi-trailer.” This is the closest available category to “semi.” Analyze `V_TYPE=07` heavy unit trucks separately, and optionally `07+08` as a sensitivity group. Do not combine light pickups (`01`) or light delivery vehicles (`05`/`06`) into semis.

**Primary event definition:** A unique collision identified by `(C_YEAR, C_CASE)` with at least one `V_TYPE=08` vehicle. Count each collision once, even when several people or tractors are recorded. Compare fatal and injury collisions separately using `C_SEV`; report tractor vehicles involved and people killed/injured as different measures. Define “fatalities in tractor-involved collisions” across all road users separately from “tractor occupant fatalities.”

The NCDB public extracts and NCDB Online focus on fatal and injury collisions. Property-damage-only collisions require a different source or separately labeled published totals. Never present casualty-collision trends as trends in all accidents.

## Work packages and verification

1. **Acquire and log sources.** Download the missing annual NCDB files from the [official Open Government dataset](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/1eb9eba7-71d1-4b30-9fb1-30cbdab7e63a), use [NCDB Online](https://wwwapps2.tc.gc.ca/Saf-Sec-Sur/7/NCDB-BNDC/p.aspx?l=en) for available national vehicle involvement tables, and capture official tractor-trailer and commercial-vehicle collision benchmarks. The updated availability review verifies the 2024 national annual statistics report, public NCDB record files through 2021, and heterogeneous provincial 2024–2025 measures. National totals and vehicle involvements remain separate from tractor-specific event records. Review publicly downloadable national and provincial record releases before claiming a full national decade. No emails or custodian contact are part of this project. Save source URL, download date, release date, size, checksum, covered years, and dictionary version. **Verify:** no missing year in any reported comparison period; each year has a traceable source and a documented definition. Keep different measures and available periods separate. See [the website and database review](website_database_review.md).
2. **Build a reproducible collision table.** Read IDs and categorical codes as strings. Normalize code widths without conflating `NN`, `QQ`, `UU`, or `XX`; deduplicate `(C_YEAR, C_CASE)` only after marking whether any linked vehicle has `V_TYPE=08`. Keep vehicle and person tables for separate measures. **Verify:** no repeated collision key in the final collision table; annual counts reconcile with the raw records and official tables to an explained tolerance; missing and unknown rates are reported by year.
3. **Measure the trend.** First publish annual tractor-involved fatal/injury collision counts and the fatal share of casualty collisions, comparing tractors with heavy unit trucks and collisions without recorded tractors. Use 1999–2021 for context, 2012–2021 as an explicitly historical available-data decade, and 2016–2021 as the observed portion of the intended period. Examine the 2020 disruption, strict-record exclusions, unknown vehicle types and the 2019 external benchmark alternative. These are descriptive counts of this public release; sampling confidence intervals are not attached to them. Any later inferential model must state its assumptions and uncertainty. Person-level deaths and injuries use the separately validated casualty pipeline and explicit record-resolution scenarios. **Verify:** annual totals and endpoints reproduce from documented code, with no person-row multiplication; show which conclusions survive the sensitivities.
4. **Adjust for exposure.** Transport Canada's [2021 commercial-vehicle safety report](https://tc.canada.ca/en/road-transportation/motor-vehicle-safety/motor-carriers-commercial-vehicles-drivers/commercial-vehicle-safety-canada-2021-annual-report-parliament) provides **modeled tractor-trailer VKT for 2015–2021**, a closer candidate denominator than registrations. Its tractor-trailer category is >15,000 kg and its model rises through 2020, so reconcile the definition and pandemic-era assumptions before calculating an event rate from `V_TYPE=08`. Seek updated 2022–2025 tractor VKT. Statistics Canada's current [registration table 23-10-0308-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=2310030801) reaches 2025, but reports **broad weight classes, not road tractors alone**. Show registration-based rates only with a clearly labeled denominator. **Verify:** numerator and denominator year, vehicle class, unit, geography, model method, and revisions are compatible; otherwise present counts and state the limitation.
5. **Study associations.** Compare collision mix by severity, road configuration, weather, surface, month, weekday, hour, road setting, driver/vehicle factors, and jurisdiction **where these fields are available and comparable**. Use proportions among tractor-involved collisions, suitable comparison groups, and stratification or count models with exposure offsets. Document missingness and reporting changes. The NCDB tutorial describes collision-level and vehicle-level contributing factors, but these fields were absent from the live selector inspected on 2026-10-14. Verify access to a suitable factor source before planning factor-specific national results. Treat recorded conditions as associations, not causes. **Verify:** each factor is defined at collision, vehicle, or person level; model estimates and confidence intervals are reproducible; claims do not exceed observational evidence.
6. **Write findings.** Produce a short methods note, annual trend table, trend/rate charts, factor comparisons, sensitivity results, and a limitations section. **Verify:** every headline number traces to a source file and code path, and the published national benchmarks are either reproduced or any discrepancy is explained.

## Important interpretation checks

- Transport Canada's [2012–2021 commercial-vehicle fact sheet](https://tc.canada.ca/en/road-transportation/statistics-data/commercial-vehicle-collisions/2021/commercial-vehicle-collisions-canada-2012-2021) reports **all commercial-vehicle** collisions falling from 45,146 in 2012 to 36,031 in 2021, and exposure-adjusted involvement rates also falling. This is a broad benchmark, not an answer for semis alone.
- Its tractor-trailer casualty-collision table reports 197 fatal and 3,103 injury collisions in 2012, versus 169 fatal and 2,107 injury collisions in 2021. These are useful validation targets; they do not establish the 2014–2023 trend.
- The 2012–2021 fatal share among tractor-trailer casualty collisions rose from 6.0% to 7.4% while **the fatal collision count fell**. Analyze the count and conditional severity share separately; neither establishes fatal risk per kilometre. The validated 2019 XLSX yields 2,775 `V_TYPE=08` events (196 fatal, 2,579 injury) versus 2,777 in Transport Canada's table. The earlier CSV yielded 2,871 and is preserved as a baseline. This source-version repair substantially reduces the discrepancy; the residual difference prevents calling the series fully reconciled. Explicitly qualified descriptive use is supported by the validation, provided the published aggregate alternative and relevant period sensitivities are shown.
- Transport Canada's [2004–2023 road safety table](https://tc.canada.ca/en/binder/road-safety-statistics) extends **commercial-vehicle** figures through 2023. Its class and outcome definitions must be checked before comparison with the road-tractor subset.
- The inherited `Collisions/` archive stops at 2017 plus a separate 2019 file. The active selection uses legacy years 1999–2013, validated official annual workbooks 2014–2019, and official CSVs 2020–2021. The original raw files and earlier source/output baseline remain preserved; see [the source repair](source_release_update.md). See [the dataset validation](dataset_validation.md) for internal exclusions and external differences. The file named `test99_to_2019_dataset_en.csv` remains truncated and is excluded.
- The live NCDB Online tool exposes 1999–2023 and can show truck-tractor **vehicle counts and occupants injured/killed**. With Vehicle Type selected, it did not offer a unique-collision count. That online series does not by itself answer how many accidents involved a semi.
- Statistics Canada has 2024–2025 registration and offence tables and a 2024 death-cause table, but the [source review](website_database_review.md#statistics-canada-search-for-20242025-data) found no semi-collision event dataset for those years. None can fill the 2024–2025 collision gap.
- Weather, road condition, and crash configuration describe where or how collisions occurred. They do not by themselves identify why risk changed. Testing possible explanations also needs exposure data and, for driver or vehicle contributing factors, appropriate recorded-factor data.

## Initial stop condition

Do not claim a result for the intended 2016–2025 decade while 2022–2025 event records are unavailable, or present this public extract as a fully reconciled current national series. The validated event grain supports descriptive findings for the observed release and stated historical periods, with record-quality and benchmark sensitivities visible. Distinguish those findings from changes in risk per kilometre, fatalities of people, or causal explanations.
