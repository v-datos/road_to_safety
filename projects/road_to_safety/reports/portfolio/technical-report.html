Executive summary

Collision burden and fatal share peak at different times

The archived cohort contains 2,949,846 fatal and nonfatal injury collisions from 1999–2021. The fatal share is 1.62%. Friday accounts for the most reported collisions with a known weekday. Sunday has the highest fatal share. Transport Canada separately reports 2,050 people killed and 130,551 injured in 2024.

The geographic comparison shows the 2024 national fatality rate of 4.9 per 100,000 residents alongside published jurisdiction rates. Different reporting practices and small populations matter when interpreting the comparison.

01 · Timing

Late afternoon accounts for the largest volume

The 16:00 hour had the most recorded collisions. The fatal share was highest at 02:00 among known hours. The weekday and hour matrix counts both fatal and nonfatal injury collisions. Cells describe recorded events and their outcomes.

Records with an unknown weekday or hour are retained in the evidence tables. The heatmap covers 2,918,220 collisions with both fields stated.

Published annual casualty totals count people. Their data vintage and counting scope differ from the archived collision cohort.

02 · Road conditions

Alignment is associated with fatal severity

Curves with gradients have a fatal share of 3.45% among 131,584 reported collisions. The share is 1.33% among 2,063,954 on straight and level roads. Road design, speed, exposure and case mix can affect the comparison.

05 · Policy

Evaluate local interventions against measured exposure

01 · Design and evaluate lower-speed local streets

Transport Canada recorded 335 pedestrian fatalities in 2024. This national count identifies a burden and provides context for local evaluation. A Toronto quasi-experiment observed fewer pedestrian collisions after local limits fell from 40 to 30 km/h. The change relative to comparator streets was statistically uncertain.

Municipal road authorities can test lower posted speeds and street designs that support them on suitable local streets with pedestrian activity. Measure operating speeds and pedestrian injury collisions against comparable streets. Track pedestrian exposure and changes in traffic volume. Fridman et al., Toronto quasi-experiment (2020)

02 · Audit rural corridors for roadway departures

The published 2024 table classifies 956 fatal collisions as rural and 855 as urban. The archived records also show a larger fatal share on curves with gradients than on straight and level roads. The FHWA implementation guidance identifies centerline and shoulder rumble strips as treatments for specific roadway-departure crash types.

Provincial and territorial road authorities can use local crash records to select appropriate corridors and assess centerline or shoulder rumble strips. Review shoulder width, cycling access, pavement and nearby residences before installation. Compare target fatal and injury crashes per vehicle-kilometre against similar untreated corridors over multiple years. FHWA rumble strip implementation guidance

03 · Audit visibility at crossings after dark

Among reported casualty collisions with a stated hour, the fatal share is highest from 02:00 to 02:59. The national records lack site lighting and nighttime travel exposure for this comparison. FHWA lighting guidance recommends considering nighttime crash history, traffic and pedestrian activity when deciding where to improve crossing visibility.

Municipal and provincial engineers can audit crossings and intersections with documented nighttime conflicts. Assess lighting, markings, glare and sight distance together. Verify lighting performance and compare nighttime injury collisions at treated and comparable untreated sites, tracking pedestrian and vehicle volumes. FHWA proven safety countermeasure on lighting

04 · Link local exposure and collision reporting

The archived collision releases lack location and exposure. Publication notes identify preliminary figures, estimates and differences in reporting practice. Canada's Road Safety Strategy 2035 and Beyond prioritizes collection, integration and timely sharing of high-quality data.

Transport ministries, police and municipal data teams can agree on a versioned road segment key, coverage fields and harmonized outcome definitions. Preserve confidentiality through controlled access and aggregated releases. Track geocoding completeness, matched exposure coverage, reporting delay and the share of severe outcomes with a verified definition. CCMTA Road Safety Strategy 2035 and Beyond

06 · Evidence

Coverage and reproducibility

Event keys are unique within their selected source and year. The cohort excludes property-damage-only crashes. The released records lack geographic identifiers, full dates and travel exposure. Severity comparisons are descriptive. Jurisdiction rates come from the 2024 publication. The Statistics Canada geometry is simplified for display.
